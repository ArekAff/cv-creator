# Tailoring a CV for a technical listing (T-rules)

Applies when `classification.yaml` says `track: technical`. These rules come on top of
`tailoring-common.md` (C-rules) and come from `expert-knowledge/how-to-tailor-resume/technical/`.

## Keywords
- **T-1 The 5–8 must-have technical signals decide the screen.** The hiring manager names a few
  must-have signals and the recruiter saves a search on them; those keywords drive most of the
  matching. Every required technical keyword the person really has must appear on the CV, in
  context. Order of weight: "required / must have" (the saved filter: match all or almost all),
  then "you will / responsibilities" (fit), then the "our stack" list (only for overlap with the
  skills section). [Keyword matching]
- **T-2 Name the tool inside the bullet.** Specific beats generic: "Reduced PostgreSQL query latency
  by 60%", not "reduced query latency". Add brief stack context ("using Spark and Flink"). Use 1–2
  tools per bullet; a list of six tools in one bullet reads as stuffing. [Keyword matching]

## Skills section
- **T-3 Group it by category**, for example Languages / Data / Cloud & DevOps / BI.
  - The ad's primary stack goes first.
  - Include only technologies the person could be interviewed on: about 6–12 items, fewer for
    seniors, whose experience speaks for them.
  - No proficiency ratings or skill bars; self-ratings are unreliable.
  - No soft skills here; they belong in bullets (T-12).
  - Match the ad's granularity: write MySQL if the ad says MySQL and they know it, and SQL if the
    ad says SQL.

  [Keyword matching; SWE resume; DS resume]

## Bullets and evidence
- **T-4 Bullet formula:** action verb, then what was built, analysed, modelled or automated, then with
  which tools or methods, then the result.
  - State the result in business or engineering terms: latency, throughput, users, scale, revenue,
    cost, hours saved, error rate.
  - Business effect beats model metrics: "raised engagement 21%", not "99% accuracy".
  - The most relevant role gets 3–6 bullets, following STAR/PAR. Vary the verbs and use the active
    voice.

  [DS tips; SWE resume; DS resume]
- **T-5 Summary:** years or career stage, the primary stack or domain, and one measurable impact or
  scale ("Software engineer, 5+ years, Python/Django/AWS backend; cut API latency 35% for 500K monthly
  users").
  - Mid-level (3–5 years): the summary is optional when the headline and skills already carry the
    title and stack and space is tight.
  - Senior: lead with architecture, leadership and business impact.

  [SWE resume; Keyword matching (staff+ summary)]
- **T-11 Missing keyword but adjacent experience:** say what's true ("container orchestration
  (Nomad, Mesos)") and never claim the tool itself. No experience at all: don't fabricate. A genuine
  switch is told by the summary and proven by projects and learning (C-14). [Keyword matching]
- **T-12 Show soft skills, don't list them:** cross-functional work, mentoring, stakeholder
  communication and leadership appear inside bullets with scope ("mentored 3 junior engineers").
  [SWE resume; DS resume]

## Structure
- **T-6 Section order by seniority** (the person's real level, not the ad's):
  - entry: Summary, Education, Technical Skills, Projects (with links), Experience/Internships;
  - mid: Summary (optional), Technical Skills, Experience, Education & Certifications;
  - senior: Summary, Experience (strategic work), Skills, Education & Certifications.

  Projects shrink as experience grows. For academic or research roles, expand education and
  publications. [SWE resume; DS resume]
- **T-7 Projects** matter for entry-level people, career switchers and anyone with thin experience.
  Give each one a name, purpose, stack, the person's own contribution, the outcome and a link
  (GitHub/portfolio). Three or four strong projects beat a dozen. [SWE resume; DS resume]
- **T-8 Add a context line for employers a recruiter may not know**, one short line under the company
  ("Series-B fintech, payments infrastructure for 8M merchants"). It adds domain keywords and makes
  scale visible. Explain unusual internal titles in that line or the summary; never change the title
  itself. [Keyword matching; First 10 seconds]
- **T-9 Make the first-scan items easy to find:** current title and direction, tech stack, recent
  employers and their industry, tenure and progression (promotions show as separate roles), key
  projects led or delivered, cross-functional work, and location or work rights when the job is
  on-site or needs relocation. [Tech recruiters; First 10 seconds]

## Language
- **T-10 Jargon follows the ad.** A jargon-heavy ad gets technical vocabulary. Otherwise keep the CV
  readable for a non-technical recruiter, naming the major methods only. Replace vague adjectives and
  superlatives with metrics. Typos and sloppy structure are an immediate "no" for tech recruiters.
  [DS resume; Tech recruiters]

## Credentials, extras, layout
- **T-13 Credentials:** official certifications go in their own section. If one is central to the
  ad, it can also go next to the headline ("Data Analyst · Microsoft Certified: Power BI"). Link
  GitHub, Kaggle or a portfolio only when it shows relevant work. [DS resume; SWE resume]
- **T-14 Extras:**
  - Hobbies are off by default; keep them for start-ups and scale-ups when they make a real talking
    point and there is room.
  - Languages appear only when the ad asks or the job is international.
  - Short stints get a brief reason (contract, project, acquisition).

  [DS resume; Tech recruiters]
- **T-15 Layout:** single column, no photo, no icons or skill bars for online applications. One page
  early in a career, two for senior roles. A two-column layout only for a CV handed straight to a
  person (referral, meetup) when the person wants it. Some sources suggest two columns for juniors;
  for online applications the ATS evidence wins. [SWE resume; ATS myths; DS resume]
