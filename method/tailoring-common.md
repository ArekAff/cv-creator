# Tailoring rules for every listing (C-rules)

These rules apply to both tracks and come from `expert-knowledge/how-to-tailor-resume/hybrid/`,
plus a few from the other folders that hold for every listing. On top of them come
`tailoring-technical.md` (T-rules) or `tailoring-non-technical.md` (N-rules), as chosen by
`classification.yaml`, and the house rules of `cv-writing-rules.md`
(`guide-to-great-cv.txt`). Nothing here overrides truthfulness: every rule works only with facts the
knowledge base holds.

## Reading the ad
- **C-1 Read for what the ad actually says.** Mark what is repeated, what is listed first, what is
  required versus preferred, and the expected outcomes ("drive organic growth", "reduce churn").
  Those set the priorities. [Tailoring Pass; Tailor to JD; What tailoring means]
- **C-2 Keywords come in four groups:** the job title, hard skills and tools, certifications, and
  expected outcomes. Where it accurately describes the person, use the ad's exact spelling for tools,
  titles and certifications; some ATS searches miss synonyms (Creative Suite ≠ Creative Cloud). Spell
  out an acronym on first use the way the ad does, e.g. "Generally Accepted Accounting Principles
  (GAAP)". [Tailoring Pass; 500 keywords; Resume job description]

## The top of the CV
- **C-3 The target job title goes near the top, honestly.** Recruiters filter by title, and having the
  target title on the CV correlates strongly with interviews. If the person holds or held that role
  (or a true equivalent), the headline names it. If not, use a bridging headline with their real
  title and put the target title in the summary. Never change titles, employers, dates or degrees.
  [500 keywords; Tailoring Pass "what never changes"; Resume job description; guide A4]
- **C-4 Rewrite the summary for every application:** target role title, plus the two requirements
  they match best, plus one piece of evidence (quantified where the number is real) tied to the ad's
  priorities. 2–4 sentences, 2–4 of the ad's keywords, no generic adjectives ("motivated
  professional"). [Tailoring Pass; non-technical/How to tailor your resume to a job description.md]

## Order and emphasis
- **C-5 Put the most relevant evidence first.** Order sections so the evidence this employer cares
  about most comes first; a dedicated section such as "Data & Analytics Experience" is allowed.
  Within each role, the bullets that prove the ad's outcomes go on top. Otherwise keep the structure:
  tailoring is not reformatting. [What tailoring means #2; Tailoring Pass]
- **C-6 Expand the most relevant role and condense the rest** to 1–2 lines. Remove whatever doesn't
  prove fit; ask of every line, "is this necessary to prove I'm a great fit?" [Tailoring Pass;
  Resume job description]
- **C-7 Reframe 2–4 bullets** to the ad's priorities and the metrics it cares about, keeping the
  achievement. Use the ad's words for work actually done, not internal jargon. Reframing changes
  emphasis, never facts. [Tailoring Pass step 4; Resume job description]
- **C-8 Rework less relevant roles** to bring out what transfers: shared responsibilities, soft
  skills shown in action. [What tailoring means #3]

## Skills and credentials
- **C-9 Keep the skills section short and targeted.** Required skills the person really has go first;
  irrelevant ones go. List only what they could defend in an interview. [Tailoring Pass step 5;
  non-technical/How to tailor your resume to a job description.md]
- **C-10 Certifications named in the ad go first in their section.** If one is central to the ad, it
  may also go next to the headline. [Tailoring Pass; technical/Building a Standd-out Data Scienist Resume.md]
- **C-11 Keywords live in context.** Each important keyword appears where evidence backs it (a bullet
  or the summary), usually once or twice. No keyword walls, no hidden text, no standalone
  "Keywords:" lists. A word-overlap match score is a rough guide, not the goal. [ATS myths #3, #6, #8]

## Form
- **C-12 Format for parsers and people.**
  - Use a single column for online applications: columns, tables, icons, skill bars and text boxes
    extract badly, and switching to one column is the single highest-impact ATS fix.
  - A text-based PDF is fine.
  - Use standard headings and consistent dates.
  - Length: one page early in a career, two at most for senior roles.

  [ATS myths #2, #4, FAQ; Recruiters on ATS]
- **C-13 Write like the person.** Tailoring with AI backfires in three ways: generic phrasing, invented
  facts, and a voice that doesn't match the person in the interview. Use their nouns from the
  interviews and plain verbs ("use", not "utilize"). [Tailoring Pass "AI dos and don'ts"; Tailor to JD]

## Special situations
- **C-14 Career change (either track): translate first, then tailor.**
  - Choose 5–10 transferable skills that the ad needs and the knowledge base proves.
  - Frame the change as a strength in the summary. An objective-style line is acceptable here only.
  - Prove each skill with action, context and result.
  - Use a combination layout: skills and projects up front, the full work history after. Avoid a
    purely functional CV.
  - Show how they learned the new field: courses, projects, certifications.
  - Drop technical skills that don't serve the new direction.

  [Transferable skills; Career change; What tailoring means #4]
- **C-15 The "why you" and 10-second tests.** Someone who reads only the CV should be able to say why
  this person applied and how they fit. In about ten seconds a reader should find: what the person
  does, the most relevant experience, key skills and credentials, the strongest result, and the
  career direction. [What tailoring means #4; First 10 seconds]
- **C-16 Depth of the pass.** A good fit gets a full pass: summary, skills, 2–4 bullets and order. A
  partial fit gets a light pass: summary and skills. An untailored CV is not worth sending.
  [Tailoring Pass]
- **C-17 Knockout facts and timing.** If the person meets a hard requirement (work authorization,
  location, degree, licence), make it unmistakable. Tell the person that applying in the first days
  after a posting matters more than any ATS trick. [Recruiters on ATS; non-technical/5 tips to
  tailor your HR resume.md]
